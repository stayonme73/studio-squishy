## Canonical rule (Scout)

**Treat this as the canonical Studio Host.** Future artwork should depict the **same individual** with consistent facial features, proportions, hairstyle, and expression. Variations should be limited to pose, environment, and approved wardrobe changes.

Do not regenerate or reinterpret the character for each new image. Use this approved reference as the canonical model. Future artwork should depict the **same person**, not a similar person.

## Art style (LOCKED)

**Maintain:**

- Modern hand-painted digital illustration
- Editorial illustration quality
- Matte painted finish
- Painterly brushwork
- Soft brush transitions
- Natural painted shadows
- Subtle canvas/paper texture
- Realistic proportions
- Warm, approachable expression

**Avoid:**

- Pixar
- Plastic CGI
- Hyper-realistic rendering
- Comic-book halftones
- Anime
- Chibi proportions
- Exaggerated facial features
- Heavy outlines

---

**Maintain:**

- Open body language
- Looking toward the customer or interaction
- Guiding, not performing

In product UI (e.g. Studio Lobby): she is a **guide**, not the primary clickable element. The podium remains the primary interaction.

---

## Why this matters

This is bigger than artwork.

Companies like Apple, Disney, and Duolingo keep characters or visual identities recognizable across years. Outfits or settings may change; people still instantly know who they’re looking at.

If every future image gives her a different face, hairstyle, or proportions, recognition erodes. This standard protects that investment.
